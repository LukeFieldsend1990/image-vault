## J3 · Four signals, one flag: inside Deep Scan

*Format: Carousel (one slide per signal + coverage ladder) · Image: new capture
of the detection-layer cards on `/product/deep-scan`*

Deep Scan, one level down. Every candidate a sweep finds is scored on four
independent signals:

**1 · Identity** — is this the performer? Matched against a reference gallery
built from their own vault scan packages, not scraped photos.

**2 · Synthesis** — is the media AI-generated? Embedded provenance markers plus
artifact analysis.

**3 · Derivation** — was it built *from* vault imagery? A perceptual-hash index
over every reference still.

**4 · Fingerprint** — did licensed scan data leak? Watermark bits carried in
every delivery, unforgeable by design.

A flag needs likeness **and** synthesis-or-derivation evidence together. One
signal alone is never a case.

And the loop we're proudest of: coverage is scored per performer — unanchored →
baseline → anchored → fortified. Every scan package archived measurably
strengthens detection. Mesh-only package with no photographic stills? We render
turntable stills from the mesh itself, so premium scans anchor too.

One honest caveat, in the product and here: "fortified" describes reference
quality, not a promise to catch everything. Detection earns trust by being
straight about its limits.

The full breakdown: https://imagevault.ai/product/deep-scan

## J4 · The burden of proof is on the wrong side

*Format: Text*

Why are deepfakes a working problem for performers — today, not in some
speculative future?

Because performers are exposed three ways at once:

**Their faces are public in high quality.** Decades of press photos, 4K film
frames, interviews from every angle. The training set for a convincing fake is
already out there.

**Their faces are worth money.** An endorsement they never gave. A scene they
never shot. A "leaked" clip that trades on their name. The fake doesn't need to
fool everyone — just enough people for one news cycle.

**The burden of proof lands on them.** Making a fake takes minutes. Proving a
negative — "I never said that, I was never there" — can take a legal team
weeks. That asymmetry is the real problem.

The answer isn't panic, and it isn't hoping platforms fix it. It's structure:
know where the authentic data lives, watch for what trades on it, and keep
evidence that stands up when it matters.

Custody first. Detection second. Receipts always.

That's the order we built in: https://imagevault.ai/product
